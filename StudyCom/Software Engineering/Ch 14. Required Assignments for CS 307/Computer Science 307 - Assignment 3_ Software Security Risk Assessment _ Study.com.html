About this Project

After developing your software feature, it's crucial to recognize and address potential security challenges. This assignment asks you to evaluate how your feature from Assignment 2 may fail or be exploited throughout the SDLC, and how emerging technologies could influence both the risks you identify and the mitigation strategies you select.

Course Learning Outcomes

The following course learning outcomes are assessed in this project:

  • Analyze security risks throughout the software development lifecycle
  • Explain the relevance and potential impact of emerging technologies on software engineering practices
  • Apply project management and software management methodologies and best practices

AI may be used as a tool to support your process with proper documentation but may not create or write any part of your project for you. AI-generated projects will result in an academic integrity violation as outlined in the Study.com Academic Integrity Policy.

Instructions

Building on your previous design and prototype implementation in Assignment 2, you will examine the security and risk implications of your earlier design and implementation choices. You will also explore how emerging technologies influence modern software engineering practices and your project's security posture. This assignment emphasizes how software can fail over time, how security issues arise at different points in the SDLC, and how emerging tools and technologies affect the development of secure, reliable systems.

Tasks

  • Identify Security Risks Across the SDLC
    • Using the same feature you designed and prototyped in the previous assignment:
      • List 4 to 6 realistic vulnerabilities or weaknesses related to your feature or its underlying components.
      • Identify the SDLC phase (requirements, design, implementation, testing, deployment/operations) where each risk emerges.
      • In 200 to 300 words, provide a rationale explaining why this risk is realistic based on your specific design decisions, prototype implementation, or assumptions. Then, assess the potential impact if the vulnerability were exploited.
  • Recommend Mitigation Techniques
    • For each risk:
      • Propose one practical mitigation strategy (e.g., secure design practice, automated testing, input validation, encryption, etc.).
      • Explain how the mitigation fits into the SDLC and how it could be supported by QA processes.
      • Incorporate at least one emerging technology that could enhance security, QA, or monitoring (e.g., AI-assisted code review, vulnerability scanners, etc.).
      • Support your recommendations with external sources (e.g., industry standards, case studies, academic references).
  • Emerging Technologies Analysis
    • Write 200-250 words explaining:
      • How at least one emerging technology (AI coding assistants, infrastructure-as-code, automated security testing tools, etc.) could affect the security, reliability, or maintainability of your feature.
      • The benefits and risks of using this technology in your project.
      • How adopting this technology might change the SDLC activities or mitigation strategies you proposed.
  • Personal Reflection
    • Write a 200- to 300-word reflection addressing:
      • What was the hardest security challenge to identify across the SDLC
      • What QA or implementation challenges might arise when applying your mitigation techniques?
      • Which vulnerabilities remain difficult to mitigate and why?
      • How emerging technologies may help or complicate dealing with long-term system failures or evolving security threats.

You may refer to the course material for supporting evidence, but you must also use 1 external sources. All sources should be cited using APA format.

Formatting

Please write your project in APA format and submit it as a Word/PDF/Google Docs file.

Grading

Your project will be graded based on the following rubric:

Criteria Excellent (5) Good (4) Needs Improvement (2-3) Unacceptable (1) Total Possible Points
Identification & Analysis of Security Risks (x4) Risks are clearly defined, realistic, well-justified, and clearly linked to SDLC phases. Risks are clearly defined, mostly realistic and justified with minor gaps. Risks are vague, unclearly defined, or unrealistic; SDLC links weak or inconsistently justified. Risks are not defined or are unrealistic and unclear. 20
Mitigation Strategies (x6) Mitigation strategies are clear, practical, SDLC-aligned, and effectively integrate emerging technologies. Mitigation strategies are mostly clear, practical, and SDLC-aligned; integration of emerging technologies is adequate. Mitigation strategies are partially unclear or impractical; limited SDLC fit or weak emerging technology integration. Mitigation strategies are missing or largely unclear and impractical. 30
Emerging Technology Impact Analysis (x3) Analysis is insightful, clearly addressing benefits, risks, and SDLC implications. Analysis is clear and mostly addresses key benefits, risks, and SDLC implications. Analysis is unclear or underdeveloped in terms of benefits, risks, and SDLC implications. Analysis is missing or largely unclear and irrelevant without meaningful evaluation. 15
Reflection & Insight on Security Challenges (x2) Reflection is insightful, addressing challenges and limitations with depth and clarity. Reflection is clear and identifies relevant challenges. Reflection is partially unclear with limited insight or shallow observations. Reflection is missing or largely unclear and lacking meaningful insight. 10

Grading Timeline & Policies:

  • You should receive your project grade within 48 hours.
  • If you are not satisfied with the score you receive, you may revise and resubmit for grading.
  • Keep in mind that the grade you receive on your project is only a portion of your overall grade for the course. Please see the course syllabus for a more detailed breakdown of the grading policy.
